# Marital Laws, Divorce, Maintenance, and Domestic Violence in India

## Governing Laws:
- Hindu Marriage Act, 1955
- Special Marriage Act, 1954 (inter-religion marriages)
- Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937
- Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (DV Act)
- CrPC Section 125 / BNSS Section 144 (Maintenance)
- Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956

---

## Grounds for Divorce

### Under Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (Section 13):
Both husband and wife can seek divorce on these grounds:
1. Adultery
2. Cruelty (physical or mental)
3. Desertion (for at least 2 continuous years)
4. Conversion to another religion
5. Unsoundness of mind (continuous or intermittent, for 3+ years)
6. Virulent and incurable leprosy (2+ years)
7. Communicable venereal disease
8. Renunciation of the world
9. Not heard of for 7+ years (presumed dead)

### Additional grounds for wife only (Section 13B):
- Rape, sodomy, or bestiality by husband
- If husband has more than one wife living
- Non-maintenance by husband for 2+ years before petition

### Divorce by Mutual Consent (Section 13B):
- Both parties can file jointly
- 6 months waiting period (can be waived by courts in some circumstances — Supreme Court ruling 2023)
- Must show separation for at least 1 year

## Maintenance / Alimony

### Section 125 CrPC / Section 144 BNSS — Maintenance for Wife and Children:
- A wife who is unable to maintain herself is entitled to maintenance from her husband
- Even if the wife is EARNING, she can claim maintenance if her income is insufficient to maintain the "lifestyle she was accustomed to in her matrimonial home"
- Courts look at: Husband's income, assets, wife's income, children's needs, lifestyle during marriage

### Key Case Law:
- **Rajnesh vs Neha (2020):** Supreme Court set guidelines for maintenance. Wife need not prove inability to maintain herself; court will consider standard of living during marriage
- **Shamima Farooqui vs Shahid Khan (2015):** Maintenance cannot be denied just because wife is employed

### How Much Maintenance:
There is no fixed formula. Courts typically award 15-25% of husband's net monthly income to wife, plus separate amounts for children.

### Interim Maintenance:
You can apply for temporary maintenance while the main case is pending. Courts typically grant this within a few months of filing.

Where to file: Family Court in the district where you reside or where the husband resides.

## Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (DV Act)

### Who is Protected:
Any woman in a domestic relationship — married, live-in, relationship of the nature of marriage, daughter, mother, sister in a shared household.

### What Constitutes Domestic Violence (Section 3):
1. Physical violence — hitting, pushing, kicking, burning
2. Sexual violence — forced intercourse, sexual abuse
3. Verbal and emotional abuse — insults, humiliation, threats, controlling behavior
4. Economic abuse — denying money for household needs, destroying assets, preventing from working

### Key Right: Right to Reside in Shared Household (Section 17):
You DO NOT need to file for divorce to claim your right to stay in the matrimonial home.
You can file a DV complaint specifically to get a "residence order" allowing you to stay.

The husband's family CANNOT throw you out of the house where you were residing, even if it is in the husband's or in-laws' name.

### Orders Available Under DV Act:
1. **Protection Order:** Preventing the abuser from committing further violence
2. **Residence Order:** Allowing you to continue staying in the shared household
3. **Monetary Relief:** Compensation for losses, medical expenses
4. **Custody Order:** Temporary custody of children
5. **Compensation Order:** Damages for injuries suffered

### How to File a DV Complaint:
1. Approach a Protection Officer (available in every district)
2. File complaint at the local Magistrate Court (Judicial Magistrate First Class)
3. Police can also file on your behalf
4. You can also approach the court directly under Section 12 of the DV Act

Emergency help: National Helpline for Women — 181 (free, 24x7)

## Triple Talaq (Instant Talaq) — Now a Criminal Offense

**Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Marriage) Act, 2019:**
Triple Talaq (saying talaq three times in one sitting) is now illegal and a criminal offense.
Punishment: Imprisonment up to 3 years + fine.
Wife can claim maintenance and custody during the pendency of the case.

Supreme Court ruling: **Shayara Bano vs Union of India (2017)** declared triple talaq unconstitutional as it violates Articles 14 and 21.

## Matrimonial Property Rights

### Streedhan:
Gifts and property received by a woman at the time of marriage (from parents, in-laws, or others) belong solely to the wife. It is NOT joint family property.

### HUF Property:
After the **Hindu Succession Act (Amendment) 2005**, daughters have EQUAL rights as sons in ancestral/HUF property. This applies even if the father died before 2005.

Key case: **Vineeta Sharma vs Rakesh Sharma (2020):** Supreme Court confirmed daughters' equal rights in ancestral property irrespective of whether the father was alive on the date of the amendment.

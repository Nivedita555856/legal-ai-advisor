# Employment Contracts, NDAs, and Workplace Rights in India

## Governing Laws:
- Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Section 27)
- Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946
- State Shops and Establishment Acts
- Payment of Wages Act, 1936
- Constitution of India Article 19(1)(g)

---

## Non-Compete Clauses — LARGELY UNENFORCEABLE IN INDIA

### Section 27, Indian Contract Act 1872:
"Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void."

### What this means:
A clause in your employment contract or NDA that says ANY of the following is VOID:
- "You cannot join any competitor for 1 year after leaving"
- "You cannot work in the IT industry for 2 years"
- "You cannot solicit our clients for 18 months"
- "You cannot start a business in the same domain for 3 years"

### Key Case Law:
- **Superintendence Company of India vs Krishan Murgai (1981):** Supreme Court held post-employment non-compete clauses are void under Section 27
- **Niranjan Shankar Golikari vs Century Spinning (1967):** Restrictions during employment are valid, but those taking effect AFTER employment ends are void
- **Wipro Ltd vs Beckman Coulter (2006):** Delhi High Court: "A blanket restraint clause is per se void"

### What CAN be enforced:
1. **Confidentiality during employment:** You cannot share trade secrets while employed
2. **Non-disclosure of specific formulas, code, or client data:** Even after leaving, for a reasonable period
3. **Non-solicitation (during employment only):** Cannot poach colleagues while still employed

### What CANNOT be enforced:
1. Any clause preventing you from getting a job in your field
2. Geographic restrictions on employment
3. Industry-wide bans after leaving

## Notice Period — Rights and Obligations

### Standard Notice Periods:
Under Industrial Employment (Standing Orders), notice periods are typically 1 month for permanent employees.

Your employment contract may specify 30, 60, or 90 days.

### If You Leave Without Serving Notice:
- The employer can only deduct salary equivalent to the unserved notice period
- They CANNOT:
  - Withhold your experience letter
  - Withhold your Form 16 (income tax document)
  - Withhold your PF (Provident Fund) settlement
  - Give a bad reference without factual basis (defamation risk)
  - File a criminal complaint for breach of contract (employment is civil, not criminal)

### Key Principle:
Payment in lieu of notice: Either party can pay the equivalent salary instead of serving the notice period. This is a standard practice.

### If Employer Refuses Experience Letter:
- Send a legal notice citing "deficiency of service"
- File complaint with the Labour Commissioner
- File complaint under State Shops and Establishment Act
- In extreme cases, file a writ petition in the High Court

## Salary Withholding — Illegal Under Law

### Payment of Wages Act, 1936:
Section 5: Wages must be paid before the 7th or 10th of the following month.
Section 8: Deductions from wages are only allowed for specific reasons (absence, advance payments, damage caused).

### An employer CANNOT withhold:
- Your last month's salary
- Your leave encashment
- Your PF contributions
- Your gratuity (after 5 years of service)
- Your experience letter

### What to do if salary is withheld:
1. Send a written demand via email and WhatsApp (evidence)
2. File complaint with Labour Commissioner (free process)
3. File complaint under Payment of Wages Act before the Authority appointed under the Act
4. File complaint with EPF Organization for PF issues: https://epfindia.gov.in
5. File Small Claims suit in Civil Court for amounts below Rs 20 Lakhs

## NDA — What is Legally Binding

### What an NDA CAN protect:
1. Specific trade secrets (formula, algorithm, proprietary process)
2. Client lists and pricing (for a reasonable period, typically 1-2 years)
3. Unreleased product information
4. Business strategy documents

### What an NDA CANNOT protect:
1. General professional skills and knowledge
2. Publicly available information
3. Information you already knew before joining
4. Information obtained independently after leaving
5. Your ability to practice your profession (Article 19(1)(g))

## Key Workplace Rights

### Gratuity (Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972):
- You are entitled to gratuity after completing 5 years of continuous service
- Calculation: (Last drawn salary / 26) x 15 x Number of years of service
- Must be paid within 30 days of leaving service

### Provident Fund (Employees' Provident Funds Act, 1952):
- 12% of your basic salary + 12% from employer is deposited monthly
- You can withdraw 75% of PF balance after being unemployed for 1 month
- Full withdrawal after 2 months of unemployment

### Sexual Harassment (POSH Act, 2013):
- Every company with 10+ employees must have an Internal Complaints Committee
- Complaints must be resolved within 90 days
- File complaint at: SHe-Box Portal https://shebox.nic.in

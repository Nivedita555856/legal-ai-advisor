# Constitutional Rights of Indian Citizens — Article 14, 19, 21

## Overview
The Constitution of India guarantees fundamental rights to every citizen. Three articles are especially relevant in everyday legal disputes.

---

## Article 14 — Right to Equality

Article 14 states: "The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."

### What this means in practice:
- A landlord cannot arbitrarily charge different rent to tenants of same category
- An employer cannot discriminate in salary, promotion, or termination without valid reason
- A bank cannot refuse services based on arbitrary criteria
- Any State action that is arbitrary, unreasonable or discriminatory violates Article 14

### Key Case Laws:
- **E.P. Royappa vs State of Tamil Nadu (1974):** "Equality is antithetic to arbitrariness"
- **Maneka Gandhi vs Union of India (1978):** Expanded Article 14 to strike down unreasonable state action
- **Shayara Bano vs Union of India (2017):** Triple talaq struck down as arbitrary and violating Article 14

### How to invoke Article 14:
File a Writ Petition under Article 32 (Supreme Court) or Article 226 (High Court)

## Article 19(1)(g) — Right to Practice any Profession

Article 19(1)(g) guarantees the right to practice any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business.

### What this means in practice:
- A non-compete clause that prevents you from working in your industry after leaving a job is LARGELY UNENFORCEABLE in India
- An NDA can protect trade secrets but cannot block your fundamental right to earn a living
- Employers cannot contractually bind employees from joining competitors if it violates this right

### Key Case Laws:
- **Superintendence Company of India vs Krishan Murgai (1981):** Supreme Court held that post-employment non-compete clauses are void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act read with Article 19(1)(g)
- **Niranjan Shankar Golikari vs Century Spinning (1967):** Reasonable restrictions during employment are valid, but post-employment are void

### Practical Implication:
If your employment contract says "You cannot join any IT company for 2 years after leaving," this clause is VOID and UNENFORCEABLE under Indian law.
Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872: "Every agreement by which anyone is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void."

## Article 21 — Right to Life and Personal Liberty

Article 21 states: "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."

### Expanded Interpretation includes:
- Right to Shelter (basic housing is a right)
- Right to Livelihood
- Right to Speedy Trial (long delays in courts violate Article 21)
- Right to Basic Amenities — electricity, water cannot be illegally cut off
- Right to Privacy (K.S. Puttaswamy vs Union of India, 2017)
- Right to Health

### Key Case Laws:
- **Olga Tellis vs Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985):** Right to livelihood is part of right to life. Eviction from pavement without notice violates Article 21
- **Hussainara Khatoon vs State of Bihar (1979):** Speedy trial is a fundamental right under Article 21
- **K.S. Puttaswamy vs Union of India (2017):** Right to Privacy is a fundamental right
- **Chameli Singh vs State of UP (1996):** Right to shelter is a fundamental right

### How landlords violate Article 21:
- Cutting off electricity/water to force a tenant to vacate is a criminal offense under the Electricity Act 2003 and also violates Article 21
- Locking out a tenant without due process violates Article 21

### Where to file a complaint:
- Police complaint (cutting off utilities = criminal offense)
- High Court Writ under Article 226
- Consumer Forum for service interruption

## Using Constitutional Rights in Everyday Disputes

### Checklist for invoking Constitutional Rights:
1. Identify the arbitrary action (landlord discrimination, employer restriction, government denial)
2. Identify the Article violated (14 for discrimination, 19 for profession, 21 for life/liberty)
3. Serve a legal notice citing the Article
4. File Writ Petition if notice is ignored

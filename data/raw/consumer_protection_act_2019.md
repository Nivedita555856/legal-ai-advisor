# Consumer Protection Act, 2019 — Complete Guide

## Governing Law: Consumer Protection Act, 2019
Replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. Came into force: July 20, 2020.

## Key Sections

### Section 2(47) — Unfair Trade Practices
Includes:
- False advertising ("Only 2 left" when thousands are available — Dark Patterns)
- Misleading price comparisons
- Fake reviews and endorsements
- Bait and switch tactics
- Charging for unordered goods
- Pyramid schemes

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has officially banned "Dark Patterns" through guidelines issued in November 2023.

### Section 2(6) — Definition of Consumer
You are a consumer if you buy goods or services for personal use (not for commercial resale).
Online purchases are explicitly included under the 2019 Act.

### Section 35 — Who Can File a Complaint
- The consumer themselves
- Any registered consumer association
- The Central Government or State Government
- One or more consumers on behalf of all (class action)

### Section 69 — Mediation
Before proceeding to a formal hearing, courts can refer disputes to Mediation to resolve them faster.

### Section 88 — Penalties for False Complaints
Filing a frivolous complaint: Fine up to Rs 10,000.

## E-Commerce Liability (New in 2019 Act)

### Platform Accountability:
Under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020:
- Amazon, Flipkart, Myntra are equally responsible if a seller on their platform sells fake or defective products
- The platform CANNOT simply say "sue the seller"
- The platform must have a grievance officer in India
- Complaints must be resolved within 48 hours (acknowledgment) and 1 month (resolution)

### Your Rights When Buying Online:
1. Right to clear product information (country of origin, expiry date, price breakdown)
2. Right to return/refund as per the return policy
3. Right to file complaint against platform if seller is unresponsive
4. Right to cancel order at any time before delivery (no cancellation fee can exceed actual cost of cancellation)

## The 21-Day Deemed Admission Rule

Under Section 36(1): If the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission does not decide the admissibility of your complaint within 21 days of filing, it is DEEMED ADMITTED.

This is a powerful protection — the court cannot simply sit on your complaint.

## Complaint Filing — Jurisdiction

| Purchase Value | Where to File |
|---|---|
| Up to Rs 50 Lakhs | District Consumer Forum |
| Rs 50 Lakhs to Rs 2 Crore | State Consumer Commission |
| Above Rs 2 Crore | National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) |

Online filing: https://edaakhil.nic.in (free online filing)
National Consumer Helpline: Dial 1915 or visit https://consumerhelpline.gov.in

## Key Consumer Rights Under the Act (Section 2(9))

1. Right to Safety — protection from hazardous goods
2. Right to Information — complete accurate product information
3. Right to Choose — competitive prices, no monopoly forcing
4. Right to be Heard — complaints must be heard
5. Right to Seek Redressal — compensation for defects
6. Right to Consumer Education — awareness of rights

---

## Dark Patterns (CCPA Guidelines, November 2023)

The government has banned these practices:
- Fake countdown timers ("Offer expires in 10 minutes")
- Forced subscription (making it hard to unsubscribe)
- Disguised advertisements
- Confirm shaming ("No thanks, I don't want to save money")
- Interface interference (pre-ticked boxes for insurance/add-ons)
- False urgency ("Only 1 left in stock" when many are available)

Report dark patterns to: https://gama.gov.in (Government Advertising Monitoring Authority)
Or file at: https://consumerhelpline.gov.in

## Bank Liability for Unauthorized Transactions

If you report an unauthorized bank transaction within:
- 3 working days: Zero Liability (bank bears full loss)
- 4-7 days: Liability limited to Rs 5,000 to Rs 10,000
- After 7 days: Liability shared proportionally

File complaint at: RBI Banking Ombudsman — https://cms.rbi.org.in
This is per RBI Circular on Customer Protection (July 6, 2017).

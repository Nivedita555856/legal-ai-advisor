# Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 — Complete Guide

## Governing Law: Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996
Amended significantly in 2015, 2019, and 2021.
Based on UNCITRAL Model Law on International Commercial Arbitration.

---

## What is Arbitration?

Arbitration is a private, binding dispute resolution process where parties agree to resolve their dispute outside the regular court system before a neutral third party called an Arbitrator.

### When is Arbitration Used:
- Commercial contracts with arbitration clauses
- Construction disputes
- Real estate transactions
- Employment agreements
- International trade contracts
- Insurance disputes

---

## Arbitration Clause in Contracts

### Is the Arbitration Clause Binding?
If your contract contains an arbitration clause like "Any dispute arising from this agreement shall be resolved by arbitration in [City]," you MUST go to arbitration before approaching a civil court.

The court will refer the case to arbitration under Section 8 of the Act.

### Can an Arbitration Clause be Challenged?

Yes, under certain circumstances:
1. **Null and void, inoperative, or incapable of being performed:** The arbitration clause itself is invalid
2. **Location causing hardship:** If the contract says "Arbitration in Mumbai" but you are in Delhi and it causes oppression/injustice, you can challenge under Section 20 (arbitrator can decide the place)
3. **Lack of jurisdiction:** If the arbitrator exceeds their authority

Section 20: The arbitral tribunal may, unless otherwise agreed by the parties, meet at any place it considers appropriate for holding hearings.

Key case: **M/s TRF Ltd vs Energo Engineering Projects Ltd (2017):** Arbitration clause where one party appoints the sole arbitrator was struck down as violating neutrality.

## 12-Month Timeline for Arbitration

### Section 29A (Inserted by 2015 Amendment):
The arbitral tribunal MUST make the award within 12 months from the date of completion of pleadings.

This can be extended by:
- Mutual consent of parties (6 more months)
- Court order (if parties are not to blame for delay)

### Fast Track Arbitration (Section 29B):
Parties can agree to fast track arbitration which must be completed in 6 months. No oral hearings — only documents. Single arbitrator.

## Key Sections

### Section 8 — Referral to Arbitration by Court:
If you file a lawsuit in civil court, and the other party says "we have an arbitration clause," the court MUST refer the dispute to arbitration (unless the court finds the clause null and void).

### Section 9 — Interim Measures by Court:
Even before or during arbitration, you can approach the court for urgent interim relief:
- Injunction to prevent disposal of assets
- Appointment of receiver
- Preservation of evidence

### Section 11 — Appointment of Arbitrator:
If parties cannot agree on an arbitrator, the Supreme Court or High Court appoints one.

### Section 34 — Challenging an Arbitral Award:
You can challenge an arbitral award within 3 months of receiving it on limited grounds:
1. Party was incapacitated
2. Arbitration agreement was invalid
3. No proper notice was given
4. Award is beyond scope of arbitration
5. Award is contrary to public policy of India

### Section 36 — Enforcement of Award:
An arbitral award is enforced like a court decree under the Civil Procedure Code.

## Domestic vs International Arbitration

### Domestic Arbitration:
Both parties are Indian. Governed by Part I of the Act. Courts in India have full supervisory jurisdiction.

### International Commercial Arbitration:
At least one party is foreign or the subject matter is international. Governed by both Part I and Part II (New York Convention). 

### Institutional Arbitration (Recommended):
Instead of appointing an ad-hoc arbitrator, you can use established arbitration institutions:
- Mumbai Centre for International Arbitration (MCIA): https://mcia.world
- Delhi International Arbitration Centre (DIAC)
- Indian Council of Arbitration (ICA): https://icaindia.com
- Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC): https://www.siac.org.sg

## Mediation under Arbitration

Under Section 69 of the Consumer Protection Act and the Mediation Act, 2023:
- Courts can refer disputes to mediation before arbitration
- Mediation is voluntary and confidential
- Successful mediation produces a "Mediation Settlement Agreement" which is enforceable as a court decree

---

## Practical Guide: What to Do When Dispute Arises

1. **Check your contract:** Does it have an arbitration clause? What city? What institution?
2. **Send a "Notice of Arbitration":** Formally invoking the arbitration clause
3. **Attempt mediation first:** Cheaper and faster than full arbitration
4. **Appoint arbitrator:** As per contract or approach High Court under Section 11
5. **File statement of claim:** Within the time specified
6. **Complete arbitration:** Target is 12 months
7. **Enforce the award:** Like a court decree under Section 36

---

## Costs of Arbitration

### Ad-hoc Arbitration:
Arbitrator's fees are negotiated. Typically Rs 50,000 to Rs 5,00,000+ depending on dispute size.

### Institutional Arbitration:
Fixed fee schedules. MCIA fees: Based on claim amount, starting at Rs 15,000 for small claims.
